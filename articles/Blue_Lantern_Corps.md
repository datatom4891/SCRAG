## Overview

The Blue Lantern Corps is a fictional organization drawing power from the blue light of hope, one of the several groups within DC Comics tied to the emotional spectrum. Created by Geoff Johns and Ethan Van Sciver, the Corps debuted in *Green Lantern* (vol. 4) #25 in January 2008. It was founded by Ganthet and Sayd, two Guardians of the Universe exiled from Oa for being willing to embrace emotion — a trait forbidden among their otherwise stoic kind.

## Origins and the Rage of the Red Lanterns

The Corps is first shown in action during the "Rage of the Red Lanterns" storyline, when the Green Lantern Corps come under assault from the newly formed Red Lantern Corps. A wounded Hal Jordan, reeling from an attack by the Red Lantern Laira, is healed by Saint Walker, who introduces himself as the Blue Lantern of Sector 1. Though fellow Green Lantern John Stewart is initially wary, Walker's ring generates a calming, personalized illusion that frees Stewart from his own residual trauma from the attack. With Stewart settled, Walker brings Jordan to the Blue Lantern homeworld of Odym, where Ganthet and Sayd ask for his help rescuing the imprisoned Sinestro from the Red Lanterns' leader, Atrocitus — Sinestro's survival, they explain, is essential to the coming War of Light.

Jordan reluctantly agrees and travels with the Blue Lanterns to the Red Lantern base on the planet Ysmault. Along the way, Walker tells him the Green Lanterns are little more than a police force and that Jordan is destined to lead the Blue Lanterns instead — while, unseen by Jordan, confiding to fellow Blue Lantern Brother Warth that turning Jordan into a Blue Lantern is essential. On Ysmault, Jordan is ambushed and captured by Atrocitus's forces just as the Sinestro Corps arrives to attempt its own rescue of Sinestro, throwing the base into three-way chaos. Walker and Warth intervene, amplifying Jordan's power and holding both warring Corps at bay — but during the fighting, Sinestro kills Laira. An enraged Jordan attacks Sinestro in response, and Laira's ownerless red ring seizes on his fury, briefly making him a Red Lantern.

Back on Odym after the battle, Jordan discovers he cannot remove a blue ring that has also attached to him, and that its presence is interfering with his own green ring. Ganthet clarifies that their earlier prophecy about Jordan "leading" the Blue Lanterns meant as a Green Lantern amplifying their power, not becoming one himself — but with the ring stuck on him and unable to be given to Walker as intended, Sayd sets out to forge Walker an entirely new one. Ganthet tells Jordan the only way to remove the ring is to genuinely find something to hope for, and sends him back to Oa — where, to everyone's surprise, even the Guardians cannot pry the ring loose. The Blue Lanterns, meanwhile, are directed to seek out wielders of the indigo light, since hope and compassion are said to need each other.

During a later confrontation with the orange-powered Larfleeze, Jordan's blue ring keeps pressing him to name what he hopes for; out of sheer frustration, he says he hopes it will simply stop asking. The ring registers this as sincere, recharges the entire Green Lantern Corps, and lets Jordan subdue Larfleeze — after which the ring finally releases him and departs to find a new bearer elsewhere.

## Blackest Night

As the Blue Lanterns clash with Larfleeze, a swarm of black power rings arrives at Odym, unable to find any corpses on the planet to reanimate and instead hovering, waiting for someone to die. Powered indirectly by Jordan's willpower, the Blue Lanterns are able to fight off Larfleeze's constructs — until his own former Orange Lantern followers return as newly created Black Lanterns and turn on him instead. Saint Walker, Ganthet, and Sayd subsequently join a coalition led by Jordan and Indigo-1 to recruit Larfleeze and Atrocitus into the fight against the Black Lantern Corps; Larfleeze only agrees after Sayd offers to become his servant in exchange.

During the climactic battle on Earth, Ganthet activates Saint Walker's ring to deputize Barry Allen (the Flash) as a temporary Blue Lantern, while the rest of the Corps works alongside the Green Lantern Corps to destroy a wave of Black Lanterns originating from the destroyed planet Xanshi.

## The New 52 and beyond

Following DC's 2011 relaunch, Kyle Rayner becomes a "magnet" drawing multiple Lantern rings to himself; when the other Corps try to reclaim their rings by force, Saint Walker is the only one of the group's representatives to instead help him escape to Oa. The plan collapses when it's revealed the other Guardians have stripped Ganthet of his emotions, and Ganthet — no longer himself — declares the Blue Lantern Corps a mistake he intends to undo.

After Walker returns to Odym, the Corps comes under attack from an alien race called the Reach. Walker sends out a desperate call for help to Kyle Rayner and the newly formed New Guardians team, and teaches his fellow Blue Lanterns to draw on their protective aura for offense as well as defense, since none of them can rely on being near a Green Lantern in battle. Ultimately, with no other options, Kyle convinces Walker to abandon Odym to the Reach entirely.

The Corps later relocates to a new homeworld, Elpis, which is soon targeted by the cosmic being Relic, intent on wiping out all "lightsmiths" in the universe. Kyle Rayner, Carol Ferris, and the Guardians attempt to help defend the planet but are unable to stop Relic from draining the Blue Central Power Battery, rendering every blue ring powerless; the rest of the Blue Lantern Corps is killed in the attack, and only an unconscious Saint Walker is rescued. Devastated by the loss, Walker's own ring abandons him — until he later regains both his hope and his ring after witnessing the abilities of a White Lantern.

## Notable members

- **Saint Walker** (Sector 1) — an alien from the dying world of Astonia who became the first Blue Lantern after helping his people find hope even as their sun failed; he later used his powers to restore it.
- **Brother Warth** (Sector 2) — a Ganesha-like alien recruiter for the Corps, later killed in the battle against Relic.
- **Brother Hymn** (Sector 3) and **Sister Sercy** (Sector 4) — early recruits, both eventually killed in the same battle against Relic.
- **Brother Shon** (Sector 11) — the first new Blue Lantern introduced after the New 52 relaunch, whose ring abandoned him mid-battle against the Reach after he lost hope, leading to his death.
- **Barry Allen** and **Hal Jordan** — each temporarily bonded with a blue ring under different circumstances.
- **Nicole Morrison** — a human host for Adara, the Blue Lantern Corps' embodiment entity.
- **Kyle Rayner**, **Guy Gardner** (in an alternate future), and **B'Dg** — additional characters who have wielded blue rings at various points.

## The oath

Like other Lantern Corps, the Blue Lanterns recite an oath to recharge their rings:

> In fearful day, in raging night,
> With strong hearts full, our souls ignite,
> When all seems lost in the War of Light,
> Look to the stars— For hope burns bright!

## Adara, the entity of hope

Adara is the emotional entity tied to the blue light of hope, depicted as a bird-like being said to have originated from the first sincere act of prayer by a being caught in a storm. Like other emotional entities, Adara is drawn to Earth and hunted by the villain Krona. It eventually bonds with a 14-year-old kidnapping survivor named Nicole Morrison, who — while possessed by the entity — confronts and forgives her abductor. Adara later plays a role in events surrounding Krona's release of the fear-entity Parallax, and, like its counterpart entities, temporarily sacrifices itself into the cosmic Source Wall during the "Green Lantern: Lights Out" storyline to help repair a damaged emotional spectrum, before eventually being freed again to help fight a later cosmic threat.

## Powers

Blue power rings run on hope, the most powerful of the seven core emotions — but hope alone isn't enough to act without willpower behind it. As a result, Blue Lanterns can only manage basic flight and a protective aura on their own; their full power requires proximity to an active Green Lantern ring. That protective aura still lets them survive in space and hostile environments, and can be extended defensively (and used to boost their own strength) even without a Green Lantern nearby. When empowered by a nearby green ring, however, a blue ring gains a green ring's full range of abilities plus healing and regeneration powers unique to the Blue Lanterns, and can actively suppress rings on the opposite end of the spectrum — red, orange, and yellow.

## In other media

The Blue Lantern Corps — including Ganthet, Saint Walker, and Brother Warth — appears in *Green Lantern: The Animated Series*. The character Razer appears as a Blue Lantern in both the *Young Justice* episode "Encounter Upon the Razor's Edge!" and in *Justice League: Crisis on Infinite Earths*. The Corps also appears in *DC Universe Online*, and Saint Walker is a playable character in *Lego Batman 3: Beyond Gotham* and *DC Legends*. Saint Walker and Brother Warth have each been produced as action figures in DC toy lines tied to *Blackest Night* and the wider Green Lantern franchise.
