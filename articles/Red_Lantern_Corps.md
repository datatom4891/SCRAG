## Overview

The Red Lantern Corps is a group of supervillains — occasionally cast as anti-heroes — powered by the emotional spectrum's red light of rage. Created by Geoff Johns and Ethan Van Sciver, the Corps first appeared in *Green Lantern* (vol. 4) #25 in January 2008. Van Sciver has said some of the group's traits were inspired by the film *28 Days Later*. Johns has described the Red Lanterns as likely the most violent of the Lantern Corps, driven purely by emotional eruption rather than any real strategy, with founder Atrocitus singled out as the only member with a coherent plan — namely, revenge against Sinestro.

## Origins

The Red Lanterns' backstory is tied to the Guardians of the Universe's first attempt at galactic law enforcement: a robotic peacekeeping force called the Manhunters. Long before the Green Lantern Corps existed, the Manhunters massacred the population of space sector 666; the few survivors banded together as the "Five Inversions," a group bent on destroying the Guardians. One of them, Atrocitus, was consumed by rage, became the first Red Lantern, and killed the rest of his own group in the process.

Because rage overwhelms the mind of most who wield it, ordinary Red Lanterns exist in a nearly feral, mindless state. Atrocitus alone has the shamanistic ability to restore a fellow Red Lantern's coherent thought without dulling their fury — an ability he first used on the character Bleez.

In *Final Crisis: Rage of the Red Lanterns*, Atrocitus is shown building a central power battery from the blood of his fallen fellow Inversions, drawing his own red ring — and the rings and batteries needed to build out the full Corps — from a resulting lake of blood. New recruits who accept his rings find their hearts rendered useless, their blood turned into a corrosive, flammable substance they're forced to expel, and their minds reduced to near-animal savagery. Once he has assembled enough followers, Atrocitus leads them in an ambush to capture Sinestro during a prisoner transfer — coincidentally the same target as a rescue attempt by the Sinestro Corps, both of which are overwhelmed by the Red Lanterns. Around the same time, the former Green Lantern Laira, expelled and convicted for murder, is given a red ring of her own while being escorted off Oa, fueling her quest for vengeance against Sinestro.

## Introduction of the full Corps and the Blackest Night era

The Corps' formation continues in the main *Green Lantern* title, where Atrocitus brings a captive Sinestro to the Red Lanterns' base on Ysmault, intending to use his blood in a ritual. Hal Jordan, aided by Blue Lantern allies Saint Walker and Brother Warth, travels to Ysmault to free Sinestro — a rescue the Guardian Ganthet believes is necessary given Sinestro's coming role in the Blackest Night prophecy. Jordan is captured by the Red Lanterns, and the arrival of the Sinestro Corps to rescue their own leader turns the standoff into a three-way battle, briefly calmed by intervening Blue Lanterns. When Sinestro is freed, he kills Laira; her enraged red ring immediately leaps to Hal Jordan instead, briefly making him a Red Lantern himself. Jordan overcomes the ring's control with help from Saint Walker's blue ring, and both the Sinestro Corps and the wounded Atrocitus retreat.

During the *Blackest Night* event, the Guardians observe the war among the various Lantern Corps, including a clash between the "Lost Lanterns" and the Red Lantern Corps over Laira's remains. As the Black Lantern Corps emerges as an eighth, death-powered faction, Black Hand's rings reanimate the dead — including Laira and the four fallen Inversions — as Black Lanterns. On Ysmault, the resurrected Inversions attack Atrocitus and tear out his heart, but the raw wrath embedded in his ring has effectively replaced the organ, keeping him alive and letting him temporarily destroy his undead former comrades. Atrocitus later joins a coalition of ring-bearers — including Hal Jordan, Sinestro, Carol Ferris, Indigo-1, Saint Walker, Ganthet, and Sayd — who need representatives of every color in the emotional spectrum to combine their light and defeat the Black Lanterns; he initially refuses to cooperate before deciding the Black Lanterns are, like the Manhunters who destroyed his home, ultimately a Guardian-made catastrophe. During the fighting, Mera is briefly deputized into the Red Lantern Corps to help hold off the Black Lantern advance, only losing the ring's hold on her once Aquaman is restored to life.

## The New 52 and later continuity

DC's 2011 line-wide relaunch, The New 52, kept the Red Lanterns' backstory largely intact while expanding on it in a new ongoing *Red Lanterns* series by Peter Milligan. In this era, Atrocitus — left adrift after failing to personally kill Krona, the being responsible for the massacre that created the Inversions — elevates Bleez to be his second-in-command, restoring her mind as he once did before. Bleez later represents the Red Lanterns within the New Guardians, a coalition of members from all seven Corps.

Later storylines see Hal Jordan send Guy Gardner undercover into the Red Lantern Corps to keep it in check; Gardner swiftly defeats Atrocitus and takes over leadership, in part because he has always felt like an outsider among the Green Lanterns. Gardner manages his own rage well enough to lead most of the Corps, while a splinter faction under Atrocitus continues more violent, vigilante-style rampages. Eventually, Jordan grants the Red Lanterns guardianship over an entire space sector — which turns out to be Sector 2814, home to Earth itself.

With the 2016 *DC Rebirth* relaunch, Atrocitus and his feline enforcer Dex-Starr return to the Corps, with Atrocitus reclaiming leadership and Bleez once again by his side. Atrocitus schemes to claim Earth as a new homeworld for the Red Lanterns by planting a "Hell Tower" that converts humans into unwitting conduits for rage, working toward the creation of a "Red Dawn."

## The Butcher

The Red Lanterns' rage is drawn from an entity called the Butcher, depicted as an Earth-like bull whose skull bears a mark resembling the Red Lantern insignia — said to have been created by the universe's first murder. Like the other emotional entities of the spectrum, the Butcher is drawn to Earth by the Life Entity and hunted at various points by the villain Krona. During an assault on Oa, Krona binds most of the emotional entities (though not Parallax) to the remaining Guardians as hosts; the Butcher is freed once Hal Jordan kills the corrupted Guardian controlling it. Later, during the "Green Lantern: Lights Out" storyline, the Butcher and its counterpart entities sacrifice themselves into the cosmic barrier known as the Source Wall to repair a damaged emotional spectrum — though a new rage entity is later born from residual anger left on Earth following the conflict with Atrocitus. The original Butcher is eventually freed from the Source Wall to help combat a later cosmic threat.

## Notable members

- **Atrocitus** — founder and (with interruptions) leader of the Corps; one of the few Red Lanterns who retains full intelligence and the ability to form constructs.
- **Bleez** — a princess abducted and tortured by the Sinestro Corps, whose vow of vengeance drew a red ring to her; she generates skeletal constructs to replace lost limbs.
- **Dex-Starr** — a formerly stray house cat whose owner was murdered; his grief and rage attracted a red ring, and he became infamous as one of the Corps' most vicious members.
- **Rankorr (Jack Moore)** — an Earth student who suppressed years of grief until police brutality against his brother pushed him past his limit.
- **Laira** — a former Green Lantern expelled for murder, later killed by Sinestro.
- **Hal Jordan, Guy Gardner, Supergirl, Mera, and Superboy-Prime** — each temporarily bonded with a red ring at different points in the mythos.
- Various other members recruited across different eras, including Skallox, Zilius Zox, Vice, and the Judge (Sheko).

## Powers and traits

Red power rings run on rage rather than willpower and typically strip their wearer of rational thought, leaving them in a feral state that only Atrocitus's ritual magic (or, for Guy Gardner, unusual force of will) can offset. As with other Lantern rings, they grant flight and survival in the vacuum of space. Red Lanterns also possess corrosive, plasma-like blood capable of breaking down other Lanterns' constructs and protective energy fields; the ring effectively replaces the wearer's circulatory system and cannot be safely removed.

The Corps' oath, recited by Atrocitus, runs:

> With blood and rage of crimson red,
> Ripped from a corpse so freshly dead,
> Together with our hellish hate,
> We'll burn you all, that is your fate!

## Reception

Critical reaction to the Red Lantern Corps has been mixed to positive. Their 2008 debut in *Final Crisis: Rage of the Red Lanterns* drew largely favorable notices, with reviewers praising the group as a strong addition to the Green Lantern mythos and noting their brutality made even the Sinestro Corps look comparatively restrained. Bleez's origin story in *Blackest Night: Tales of the Corps* #2 was also well received, particularly for its artwork. The 2011 solo ongoing series had a rockier reception, with critics generally praising the art while faulting inconsistent plotting and characterization.

## In other media

The Red Lantern Corps has appeared in numerous adaptations, including *Green Lantern: The Animated Series* (featuring the original character Razer), *Justice League Action*, *DC Super Hero Girls*, several Lego DC titles, *DC Universe Online*, and the *Injustice* fighting game series, along with a range of tie-in action figures.

## Collected editions

The *Red Lanterns* solo series and related crossover material have been collected across multiple trade paperbacks and hardcovers, including *Blood and Rage*, *The Death of the Red Lanterns*, *The Second Prophecy*, *Blood Brothers*, *Atrocities*, *Forged in Blood*, and a 2025 *Red Lanterns: The New 52 Omnibus* gathering the bulk of the era's material.
