## Overview

"Sinestro Corps War" is an 11-part crossover that ran through DC's *Green Lantern* and *Green Lantern Corps* titles between June and December 2007. Written primarily by Geoff Johns and Dave Gibbons, with art led by Ivan Reis, Patrick Gleason, and Ethan Van Sciver, the story pits the Green Lanterns of Earth — Hal Jordan, Kyle Rayner, John Stewart, and Guy Gardner — and the wider Green Lantern Corps against the Sinestro Corps, an army of fear-based soldiers assembled by the disgraced former Green Lantern Sinestro. The premise drew on a 1986 Alan Moore short story, "Tygers," published in *Tales of the Green Lantern Corps Annual* #2.

Reception was strongly positive: the event was named to several "best of the year" lists, earned an Eisner Award nomination for its art, and became a major commercial success for DC, with multiple issues going back for extra print runs. It forms the middle chapter of Geoff Johns' Green Lantern trilogy, bridging *Green Lantern: Rebirth* (2004–05) and the 2009 event *Blackest Night*.

## Plot

Following his defeat in *Green Lantern: Rebirth*, Sinestro retreats to the planet Qward in the Antimatter Universe. There he builds an army — the Sinestro Corps — recruiting beings chosen for their capacity to "instill great fear," each armed with a yellow power ring mirroring the Green Lantern Corps' own rings. His allies include the fear-entity Parallax and a resurrected Anti-Monitor. The Sinestro Corps then launches an assault against the Green Lanterns and the wider universe.

During an attack on the Green Lantern homeworld of Oa, the Sinestro Corps inflicts heavy losses and frees the imprisoned Superman-Prime and Cyborg Superman. Kyle Rayner is captured and taken to Qward, where Sinestro separates him from the entity Ion bonded to him, allowing Parallax to take possession of Kyle instead. Hal Jordan, John Stewart, and Guy Gardner attempt a rescue but are themselves trapped; a second rescue attempt splits the Green Lanterns into two groups, one of which frees Ion while the other, ambushed, is eventually saved. The survivors escape back to the normal universe and Hal, John, and Guy return to Earth to warn the Justice League that the Anti-Monitor has returned.

As Sinestro Corps members spread out to ambush Green Lanterns across the galaxy, the Guardians of the Universe make a fateful decision: they rewrite their sacred text, the Book of Oa, striking out a prophecy concerning a coming "Blackest Night" over the objections of two Guardians, Ganthet and Sayd. In its place they add new laws, the first of which authorizes lethal force against the Sinestro Corps — and Ganthet and Sayd are expelled from Oa for their dissent. The Sinestro Corps then teleport their central power battery to a new Warworld, revealing their true target: Earth.

The war reaches New York City, where Green Lanterns battle Sinestro Corps members across the planet. Hal frees Kyle from Parallax's possession, and the entity is re-imprisoned within the Lanterns' power batteries by Ganthet and Sayd. Ganthet and Sayd then reveal to John and Guy the prophecy they had tried to protect: five more Lantern Corps, each keyed to a different color and emotion, will eventually arise, leading to a "War of Light" in which all corps are destroyed — culminating in the Blackest Night.

The Guardians travel to Earth and appoint Sodam Yat as the new host of Ion. After a drawn-out battle, the Sinestro Corps is overwhelmed by superior numbers. One Guardian sacrifices himself to banish Superman-Prime to another universe; another Guardian, Scar, is badly wounded by the Anti-Monitor. Hal and Kyle capture Sinestro in Coast City. In total, 440 Green Lanterns are killed over the course of the war.

In the aftermath, the Guardians enact a second new law empowering the Corps against future threats from the Sinestro Corps. Recognizing that the Blackest Night prophecy will eventually come true regardless, Ganthet and Sayd depart Oa, forging a blue power ring with the aim of building a new corps founded on hope. Meanwhile, the wreckage of the Anti-Monitor, scattered into space during the battle, lands on a dark world and is transformed by an unknown force into a black power battery — a seed for events to come.

## Background and creative process

The storyline built directly on ideas from Alan Moore and Kevin O'Neill's 1986 short story "Tygers," incorporating its prophecy of the Blackest Night, the character Sodam Yat, the living planet Ranx, and the "Children of the White Lobe." A minor character from another Moore story, "Mogo Doesn't Socialize," makes a brief return appearance as well.

Development began in September 2006, with the project originally titled simply "Sinestro Corps" before "War" was added. Johns and Van Sciver first announced the crossover publicly at the 2006 Fan Expo Canada, describing it as "the next level" of *Rebirth*. In a later interview, Johns compared the trilogy to the original *Star Wars* films, with *Rebirth* as *A New Hope* and "Sinestro Corps War" as *The Empire Strikes Back*. The creative team also described the story as "World War II with the entire universe," and Sinestro's overhaul as a fascist-styled tyrant was explicitly shaped by parallels to Adolf Hitler and Nazi Germany.

Several longstanding *Green Lantern* villains — Superboy-Prime (renamed Superman-Prime during the event), Cyborg Superman, and the Manhunters — were folded into the Sinestro Corps' ranks, and the Anti-Monitor made his first major reappearance since his death in 1985's *Crisis on Infinite Earths*. The role of Ion passed from Kyle Rayner to Sodam Yat. *Green Lantern* #25 substantially expanded the "emotional spectrum" concept, laying out seven corps keyed to the colors of the rainbow (red for rage, orange for avarice, yellow for fear, green for willpower, blue for hope, indigo for compassion, and violet for love), plus an eighth, black, representing death — directly setting up 2009's *Blackest Night*.

## Reception and legacy

"Sinestro Corps War" turned *Green Lantern* into one of DC's best-selling titles. The opening one-shot sold out in a single day and went through four printings; several other issues also required additional print runs. Critics were highly positive: reviewers called it a "smash hit" and DC's "blockbuster event of the year," and it appeared on multiple "best of 2007" lists. DC executive editor Dan DiDio called it the best thing the publisher released that year and cited it as a model for future crossovers, including *Final Crisis*. Ethan Van Sciver received a 2008 Eisner Award nomination for his art on the opening special.

The storyline was later cited as the intended basis for the plot of a cancelled sequel to the 2011 *Green Lantern* film.

## Collected editions

The main story has been collected in two hardcover/paperback volumes and later as a single complete edition, with the four *Tales of the Sinestro Corps* one-shots and related material gathered separately as *Green Lantern: Tales of the Sinestro Corps*.
