## Overview

"Blackest Night" is a 2009–2010 crossover event built around Nekron, a personification of death who reanimates dead superheroes and villains as "Black Lanterns" in a bid to erase all life and emotion from existence. Geoff Johns, who wrote the central miniseries, has described emotion as the story's core theme. The event is the third and final chapter of the Green Lantern trilogy that began with *Green Lantern: Rebirth* and continued through *Sinestro Corps War*.

The story had been foreshadowed since the closing pages of *Green Lantern* (vol. 4) #25, where the exiled Guardians Ganthet and Sayd warn Earth's four Green Lanterns — Hal Jordan, Guy Gardner, John Stewart, and Kyle Rayner — of a prophecy: five new Lantern Corps, each tied to a color and an emotion, would eventually join the existing Green and Sinestro Corps, triggering a "War of Light" that would ultimately destroy the universe. Johns traced the prophecy's roots to Alan Moore's short story "Tygers." Additional groundwork was laid in *DC Universe* #0, which showed the villain Black Hand discovering a mysterious black power battery on the prison planet Ryut.

*Blackest Night* #0 was released for Free Comic Book Day on May 2, 2009, launching a standalone miniseries of nine issues (#0–8) alongside numerous tie-ins across the DC line, including a run of three-issue spin-off miniseries for individual characters and teams.

## Plot

The Green Lanterns Ash and Saarek discover the Black Central Power Battery hidden in a classified sector of space; on touching it, they realize something has been woken — and are killed by monstrous hands that emerge from within it. Elsewhere, the shattered remains of a destroyed planet are shown surrounded by black power rings drifting through space. In Gotham City, Black Hand steals Bruce Wayne's skull from his grave as a black power battery begins to charge.

On Oa, the Guardians realize too late that Ganthet and Sayd's warnings were correct, but the rogue Guardian Scar kills one of their own and imprisons the rest to stop them intervening. Thousands of black rings converge on the Green Lantern Corps' burial crypt, reanimating the dead as the Black Lantern Corps. Hal Jordan and a newly resurrected Barry Allen (the Flash) investigating Bruce Wayne's grave are attacked by a Black Lantern Martian Manhunter, while on Oa the living Green Lanterns are confronted by an army of their own reanimated dead.

As the Black Lanterns spread, they reanimate and kill a widening circle of heroes — among them Hawkman and Hawkgirl — who are then absorbed into the growing Black Lantern ranks themselves. The living heroes gradually realize that these revived figures are hollow imitations feeding on grief and unresolved emotion rather than the true departed. The Indigo Tribe, a previously unseen Lantern Corps built on compassion, intervenes to help the heroes and stresses that only a united alliance of all the Lantern Corps can hope to defeat the Black Lanterns.

The battle converges on Coast City, where the rogue Guardian Scar has relocated the Black Central Power Battery. Black Hand summons Nekron himself, who begins reanimating the city's dead. Hal Jordan is joined by fellow ring-bearers from across the spectrum — Carol Ferris, Sinestro, Atrocitus, Larfleeze, Saint Walker, and Indigo-1 — along with Ganthet and Sayd, as the fight for Earth escalates. To combat the overwhelming Black Lantern horde, temporary rings of each color are distributed to non-Corps allies, deputizing figures such as Lex Luthor (orange), Wonder Woman (violet), and Mera (red) alongside the returning Corps leaders.

During the climax, Nekron kills a Guardian to release a captive "Life Entity" — the true, hidden origin of all life in the universe, which the Guardians had long concealed to protect their own authority. Sinestro, consumed by grief and rage over the long-ago death of Abin Sur, seizes the Entity's power for himself, briefly becoming its host, and is killed and then revived by a white power ring in the process. Ultimately it is Hal Jordan who successfully bonds with the Entity, using its power to transform himself and the Black Lantern-afflicted heroes into a new White Lantern Corps. This act restores Black Hand to life, which in turn frees the long-imprisoned Anti-Monitor from the Black Lantern battery — Nekron drives him off before white light rings finally destroy Nekron for good.

In the story's resolution, the White Lantern's power permanently restores twelve of the dead to true, lasting life — including Aquaman, Hawkman, Hawkgirl, Martian Manhunter, and Jade — while the rest of the reanimated remain gone for good. Reunions follow: Aquaman and Mera reconcile, Hawkman and Hawkgirl embrace with restored memories of their past lives, and Superman welcomes back Martian Manhunter. Loose threads are left for future stories — including the survival of Bruce Wayne, whose corpse turns out to have been a fake, and the fate of the Life Entity itself, which Hal notes is still out there somewhere.

## Aftermath and tie-ins

*Blackest Night* was preceded by a wave of prelude stories: the "Agent Orange" arc in *Green Lantern* #39–42, and the "Sins of the Star Sapphire" and "Emerald Eclipse" arcs in *Green Lantern Corps* #29–38. Following the event's conclusion, DC launched the 25-issue biweekly series *Brightest Day*, again written by Johns alongside Peter Tomasi, to follow up on the story's unresolved threads. *Action Comics* also picked up a related plot thread, with Lex Luthor — left charged with the orange light of avarice — pursuing a quest to hunt down the scattered Black Lantern power sources.

## Reception

*Blackest Night* was well received by critics. Comic Book Resources gave the first and third issues of the central miniseries a perfect score, with the second issue close behind; IGN rated the opening issues consistently high as well. The event has since been referenced across DC media, including tie-in cosmetic content in video games such as *Injustice: Gods Among Us*, *Batman: Arkham Origins*, *DC Universe Online*, and *MultiVersus*, and it served as the basis for the single-player campaign of the mobile game *DC Legends*. An alternate, considerably darker take on the story's ending was later explored in the *Tales from the Dark Multiverse: Blackest Night* one-shot.

## Collected editions

The core series and its many tie-ins have been repackaged across numerous hardcover and paperback collections, including a 2013 *Absolute Blackest Night* edition and a 2019 tenth-anniversary omnibus gathering the central series alongside nearly all of its tie-in issues.
